The 4 durable lessons (routed to calibration)

  • 1. R16 knockout favorites hold serve more often than group-stage intuition suggests, but margins are still too narrow. 6/7 outcome leans hit, but every projected scoreline undershot the eventual margin or total by at least 1 goal in the games that broke open (Morocco 2–0→3–0, Mexico-England 1–1→2–3, USA-Belgium 1–1→1–4). → Same repeat as the 06-24/07-02 margin lesson — once single-elimination pressure forces a side to commit fully forward (chasing OR protecting), the model's calib-minute Poisson still compresses the margin more than reality does.
  • 2. A "safe" pre-tournament favorite can still get outright beaten in a straight two-way lean — don't over-trust proximity favorites in an all-or-nothing knockout. Brazil was the only outright miss: sim had Brazil the largest single probability (43.9%) but a near-toss-up field (28/28 draw/away), and Norway's Haaland won it late (79', 90'). LESSON: when a "favorite" clears 40% only by a few points over the runner-up and the draw is nearly as live, treat it as a true coin-flip for staking purposes, not a lean — the touted "favorite" tag was doing more work than the underlying probabilities supported.
  • 3. Comeback-from-2-down finishes are becoming a recurring R16/R32 theme, not a one-off. Argentina trailed Egypt 0–2 before scoring 3 in the final 13 minutes; this echoes the Belgium–Senegal late-chaos miss from 07-01. LESSON: the model's must_win_chase lever is doing real work (correctly kept Argentina's win prob at 58% pre-match) but the in-game late-goal clustering (3 goals in 13') is still underweighted for AAA-tier teams with a individual match-winner (Messi) on the bench of possibilities — consider a "elite closer" multiplier for the last-15 minutes distinct from generic must-win chase.
  • 4. Scorer-list top-pick hit rate held at a respectable 4/7, but role-player/tactical-fit misses repeat. Hakimi (Morocco fullback, assisted not scored), Yamal (teenage wide forward, nation's most-hyped scorer, didn't find the net in a 1-goal Spain win decided by a late midfield run), and De Bruyne (deployed deeper, didn't finish; De Ketelaere had the brace instead) were all reasonable top-picks that missed to a lower-listed teammate who was still on the list. LESSON: this is a genuine, calibrated coin-flip-among-a-short-list problem, not a construction-gap problem (unlike the Pépé/Amad Diallo/Porro misses) — keep scorer confidence framed as "low/medium among 2-3 plausible names," never "primary lock," in one-goal knockout games.

Infrastructure lesson (routed separately, non-football)

The self-scheduling lineup-watcher silently went dark for 4 straight match days after its last one-time fireAt fired on 07-03 — it never re-armed itself for 07-04 onward, and the morning planner's own cron kept running but produced nothing (no fixture check ran to completion). This is the single biggest process risk in the system: a scheduled task with a one-time fireAt that fails to re-arm is invisible until someone manually audits command-center.json's date field. Recommend: add a lightweight daily health-check (e.g. inside the 8 AM planner) that asserts the watcher is enabled for any day with fixtures, and treats a stale board date as a hard failure to report, not a silent gap.
