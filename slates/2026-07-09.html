1) France vs Morocco — QF · Foxborough (Gillette Stadium) · 4:00 PM ET

Prediction: France win — most likely 2–0; alts 3–0, 1–0. Draw after 90' → ET/pens live tail (not thin).

Why: France is a perfect 6-for-6 this tournament, 14 goals for and 2 against — the best defensive and attacking record left in the field, and Mbappé is tied for the Golden Boot race (7 goals) after a penalty winner against Paraguay. But that Paraguay result (1–0, decided by a 70' penalty) shows France can be slowed to a single goal by a well-organized low block, not just blown open — the R16 game was a grind, not a rout, despite the eventual final score. Morocco brings exactly that profile: they beat Canada in the R16 despite managing only five shots (fewest by a winning team in a WC knockout match since 1966), meaning their whole tournament path so far is "concede little, win with fewer chances than the opponent." They also have an established big-game gear — going the distance (extra time + penalties) earlier in the bracket, and taking a point off Brazil late in the group stage via a stoppage-time leveler. That's a side that does not fold under pressure, which keeps the draw/ET tail live even though France is the clear quality favorite. The compensating factor: Morocco's most creative attacking piece not named Diaz, Ismael Saibari, is out (hamstring, replaced 22' vs Canada) — Rahimi steps into the front line, which thins Morocco's own goal threat and tilts this toward a France-controls-the-game script rather than a genuine coin-flip. Net read: France should win, and the "France = not a bus, model the wider margin" lesson argues for 2–0 as the primary rather than a capped 1–0, but Morocco's proven resilience keeps 1–0 (grind, à la Paraguay) and the draw/ET route both live enough not to load up.

Players to watch: Mbappé (Golden Boot race, penalty duty, France's decisive/leading scorer all tournament), Doué (chance creation, was fouled for the France penalty vs Paraguay); Brahim Diaz (Morocco's primary creator, African record 4 assists this tournament), Rahimi (stepping into the central striker role in Saibari's place). Chadi Riad (Morocco CB) is a doubt after being substituted last match — a defensive-line question mark if he can't go.

Most likely scorer: Mbappé (high — tournament top scorer, penalty taker, France's clear focal point). Alts: Doué (creator turning scorer), Kolo Muani/Barcola (rotation/impact options off the bench); for Morocco, Rahimi (low-med — new to the central striker slot, first start there) is the most plausible source of a Morocco goal, with Brahim Diaz (low) as a secondary threat via a set piece or a second-phase strike.

Confidence & stake: Medium-high confidence, 2u France. Quality gap is real and the "widen the France margin" lesson pushes past a capped 1u, but the stake stops short of the top of the ladder because Morocco has repeatedly proven it can go the distance and Saibari's absence, while a real dent, doesn't erase Morocco's structural discipline (5 shots to win a knockout game is not a team that will get overrun early).

What would flip it: An early Morocco goal (their tournament pattern is "concede little, strike rarely, but when they do it changes the game plan" — see the Brazil/Netherlands legs) would flip this into the tightly-priced ET/pens game their bracket run has already shown they can win; alternatively, a Riad injury withdrawal exposing Morocco's back line early would argue for widening France's margin further (toward the 3–0 alt) rather than the 2–0 primary.
